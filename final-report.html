Rook Dispatch · Release 4.2 · Impact report

After release 4.2, pings went unanswered and four responders went quiet.

On 12 August the share of pings that went unanswered jumped overnight, and four responders stopped getting work at all. This report shows what happened, the evidence behind it, the fix I built, and the check that helps stop it repeating.

What happened

Nothing was broken before 4.2. Then it broke on release day.

Release 4.2 shipped three changes. The data shows one sharp break on 12 August, not a slow drift, which rules out seasonality.

4
X-Ray VisionReading the routing code
Finding
Points go down easily and never come back by themselves. One function adds points (record_accepted, +0.08). One removes them (record_declined, −0.12), and it treats a turn-down and a missed ping the same. There is no decay. This is one code snapshot, not yet confirmed with Wen Li.
The prompt I wrote
Please find me the part of this code that removes points when someone misses a ping or turns one down. Show it to me and explain it in plain english. Then please find me every single thing in this code that adds points to somebody's points total.
Artifact: plain-English walkthrough of the code, and a draft reply to Marcus (not sent)
5
Super SpeedPRD and phased prototype
Finding
One person tells the story. Farlight took 73% of about 12 pings a week before 4.2, got 11 pings in total after, and last took one on 14 Aug. The fix is phased: answer tickets, restore the wait and show handlers why a responder is quiet, then build a way back.
The prompt I wrote
Looks solid as MVP. Can we further refine and create another prototype selection option that could be even more refined without adding significant dev effort?
Artifact: brief.md and prototype-v2.html (shown below)
6
SidekicksA reusable review skill
Finding
The skill found real gaps in my own brief. Run on the PRD it returned "Ready with fixes" and caught contradictions such as "no routing change" alongside restoring a routing config value, and a promised date against a "no date until engineering agrees" rule. I applied nine fixes.
The prompt I wrote
I want to save a habit as something I can reuse. When I review a product brief (or PRD) before it goes any further, here's what I actually check for: - It should be in my style - It names who owns it - It describes the problem definition (problem being solved) - It discusses key constraints assumed or explicit - It says how we'll know it worked - The scope at the end matches the scope at the start - It explains the problem before it proposes a fix Turn that into a skill called review-checklist, so I can point it at any brief and get the same check every time, without me explaining it again. In addition to these items, please feel free to prompt me with suggested additions
Artifact: review-checklist skill, 14 checks

Availability Confidence was committed for 4.2 and is missing from the release notes.

One PM, no overlap, and the reason for the wait is recorded nowhere I can find.

I have not seen the 4.2 brief itself, so this table maps each gap to the check that would have forced the question. It is not a claim about what the 4.2 team did or skipped. Where it stands

What is established, what is a hypothesis, what is open.

StatusItem
EstablishedMissed pings rose from 2.3% to 18.0% on release day, 12 Aug. Turned-down pings fell. The break is not seasonal.
EstablishedThe four responders have taken no ping since 14–19 Aug. Their handlers cannot see why, and tickets are unanswered.
HypothesisThe 60s wait caused the broad rise in misses. The no-decay scoring loop and the proximity weight starved the four. Neither is confirmed until Wen Li reviews the routing and the two changes are tested separately.
OpenWhy the wait was cut (Priya, via Marcus). How ranking treats a miss and a late yes (Wen Li). Data after 6 Sep (Ravi). A call with Linda (via Nadia). Which 4.2 commitments still stand for Q3 (Helen).
OpenWhy callouts end after one missed ping: 27 callouts had a missed ping and no taker since 12 Aug, against 3 before.
